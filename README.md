## What I Built

**Heliograph** is a short 2D solar-noir platformer about light, memory, time, and a machine trapped inside a problem it cannot solve.

You are a courier who wakes with no memory on the summer solstice: the longest day of the year, the one day the sun is supposed to never quite die. A cracked handheld computer flickers on in your hand and tells you the truth: tonight the sun will set, and a relay station full of light has one unfinished message left to send before the dark.

Sunlight is your battery and your map. It refills your light cell, reveals routes, and exposes the station’s hidden language. Shadow keeps you safe from the machines, but slowly drains you, so you can never simply wait. Every screen asks you to choose when to expose yourself, when to charge, when to traverse, when to hide, and when to decode.

At the center of the game is a light-relay puzzle. Most of the station is dark. Stand in a live sunbeam, trip a relay, and the light is thrown forward: a beam snaps to the next aperture, a new path becomes visible, a cipher glyph becomes readable, and the chain continues. You are literally carrying the light deeper into the ruin, one beam at a time. Skip a relay and the road ahead stays dark.

The jam theme is the solstice: light and darkness, and the passage of time. **Heliograph** builds the entire game out of that tension:

- **Light vs. darkness is the core mechanic, not a backdrop.** Light is power, information, and danger at once; shadow is safety that costs you.
- **Time is the antagonist.** The whole game is one long solstice day bleeding into night, and the message has to leave the station before dark.
- **The station itself is a heliograph.** A real heliograph sent Morse code by flashing sunlight off mirrors. In this game, light is not just illumination — light is the message.

Finishing the puzzle and learning the next piece of the story are the same action.
